Sleeper Dynasty Basketball Team — Film Room

Cade Cunningham

Detroit Pistons · PG

Cade is the whole Detroit offense again: high pick-and-roll volume, the stepback three, and real defensive effort as a helper and even a shot-blocker. He has scored efficiently in the Pistons' strong start, including a 30-point night against Charlotte, and the reel shows him getting downhill at will. Keep: a franchise point guard in his prime, the cornerstone of this build.

Evan Mobley

Cleveland Cavaliers · PF/C

Mobley is being asked to initiate more, bringing the ball up, running inverted pick-and-roll and taking pull-ups, while still anchoring the defense. The film against Washington shows an efficient night (24 on 8-12) built on rim finishes and short-range touch, though his scoring still swings game to game. Keep: an elite two-way big still adding offensive tools.

Paolo Banchero

Orlando Magic · PF

Paolo opened with a monster game against Atlanta (35 points, 10 assists on 10-of-12 shooting), running the offense as a true point forward. He then suffered a low-grade high ankle sprain on Oct. 23 and is listed out until at least late November. Keep: a short-term absence that does not change his dynasty value, and he is a buy-low target if anyone panics.

Jabari Smith Jr.

Houston Rockets · PF/C

Jabari's opening-week reel against Dallas (30 on 11-17) shows the stretch-four game working: spot-up and pick-and-pop threes plus confident turnaround jumpers over smaller defenders. Houston's frontcourt depth caps his touches some nights, but his floor-spacing and switchable defense keep him on the court. Keep: a 3-and-D big who is still getting better.

Dylan Harper

San Antonio Spurs · PG/SG

In his second season Harper has stepped into a bigger creator role, with strong drives, pick-and-roll splits and passes on the move, and a 22-point game in San Antonio's first win. The rough nights are tape too: 4-of-17 against Utah shows the jumper is not settled yet. Keep: a second-year guard trending up, with a ceiling worth waiting on.

Keyonte George

Utah Jazz · PG/SG

George is Utah's lead scorer and has put up 20-plus in each game with film, from a tidy 24 on 13 shots against the Lakers to a 31-point, 27-shot game against the Spurs. The tape shows deep pull-up range and a willingness to take hard shots, but efficiency swings and the Jazz are losing. Keep: a young, high-usage guard on a rebuilding team, which is exactly what dynasty rewards.

RJ Barrett

Toronto Raptors · SF/PF

Barrett has scored 20-plus in every Toronto game with film so far, attacking off the catch, finishing through contact, and taking enough threes to stay a threat. The Orlando reel (25 on 9-18) is typical: a reliable volume scorer, though not someone who lifts your efficiency. Keep for now: steady production; sell if a contender's owner pays like he is a long-term core piece.

Jonathan Kuminga

Minnesota Timberwolves · PF

Now in Minnesota, Kuminga is getting real touches, and the reel against his old Golden State team shows the downhill power game and transition finishing (20 on 7-15). The jumper and decision-making are still uneven, but the role is bigger than it ever was with the Warriors. Keep: the opportunity finally matches the athletic upside.

Jarrett Allen

Cleveland Cavaliers · C

Allen is still Cleveland's starting center: rim-running, lobs from James Harden, offensive rebounds, plus a rare made three this week. No full-game reel from this season has been posted yet, so the film here is from last season's playoffs (a 23-point second-round closeout win over Detroit). Keep: a dependable rebounds, field-goal percentage and blocks anchor, though at his age his value will slowly decline, so trade him if a strong offer comes.

Deandre Ayton

Washington Wizards · C

Ayton signed with Washington and has come off the bench behind and alongside Anthony Davis and Alex Sarr, with good energy in the opener but a capped role. No full-game reel from this season has been posted yet, so the tape here is from last season's playoffs with the Lakers, which shows the efficient finishing and elbow touch he still has. Trade/hold: he scores efficiently when on the floor, but a crowded frontcourt limits his minutes.

Ryan Rollins

Milwaukee Bucks · PG

Rollins is a returning starter-level guard in the post-Giannis Bucks backcourt, but the start has been uneven, with Taylor Jenkins tightening rotations and rookies Brayden Burries and Kasparas Jakucionis pushing for minutes. He did lead a fourth-quarter comeback with 19 points against Indiana. No full-game reel from this season has been posted, so the film is his 36-point game from last March. Hold: Kevin Porter Jr. being waived helps, but he has to hold off the young guards.

Bennedict Mathurin

New Orleans Pelicans · SG/SF

Mathurin signed with New Orleans as a free agent, and the early returns have been underwhelming, with local coverage saying they confirm preseason concerns about his shot selection and defense. No full-game reel from this season has been posted, so the film is from last March (28 against the Knicks), which shows his best version: a downhill scorer who gets to the line. Trade if you can get fair value: his scoring is real, but his role in New Orleans is not secure.

Meleek Thomas

Cleveland Cavaliers · SG

Thomas is a true rookie (2026 draft class) and made his NBA debut this week with 16 points and four threes in an overtime win, drawing public praise from Donovan Mitchell afterward. The reel shows a confident shooter who is not afraid in big moments; he is now day-to-day with a foot issue, and minutes on a deep Cavs roster will not come easily. Keep: an encouraging debut for a long-term stash.

Yang Hansen

Portland Trail Blazers · C

Yang is a second-year center (2025 draft), not a rookie, and so far his minutes have come almost entirely in garbage time. The clip is every possession from his late stint against Memphis: soft touch, a made three, a block, and good passing instincts, but it is a small sample against backups. Hold as a deep stash at most, and cut him first if you need the roster spot.

Chris Cenac Jr.

Boston Celtics · PF/C

Cenac is a 2026 rookie who has not earned rotation minutes for Boston yet; the only footage posted is from pregame and practice, not games. He is a long-term development project as a big man who can stretch the floor. Hold only if your bench is deep; otherwise he is a cut candidate until he plays real minutes.

Jimmy Butler III

Golden State Warriors · SF/PF

Butler is recovering from a torn right ACL (injured in January 2026) and is targeting a return in January or February. At 37, coming off that surgery, he is a short-window asset whose value depends on how he looks when he comes back. Cut or sell: in dynasty he only makes sense for a team pushing for a title this season.

No qualifying game film right now.

Mark Williams

Phoenix Suns · C

Williams had surgery on a torn left labrum in September and has an estimated return around early February. Phoenix has been starting Oso Ighodaro in his place, so he will need to win back his role. Hold: he is still a young starting-caliber center, but with his injury history he is a stash, not a building block.

No qualifying game film right now.

Shaedon Sharpe

Portland Trail Blazers · SG

Sharpe tore his right meniscus in an August workout and was expected to miss about six months, putting his return near March. He also missed a lot of last season with a leg stress reaction, which raises concern about his durability. Keep: the athletic scoring upside of a young guard is still worth stashing, but expect very little this season.

No qualifying game film right now.

Yves Missi

New Orleans Pelicans · C

Missi is out with a left knee contusion/bone bruise, with an estimated return around Nov. 8. When healthy he is a rim-running, shot-blocking big in New Orleans' frontcourt rotation. Hold: this is a short-term injury, and his value comes from rebounds and blocks once he is back.

No qualifying game film right now.

Kevin Porter Jr.

Free agent · PG/SG

Milwaukee waived Porter on Oct. 19 to set its roster, despite his productive but injury-shortened 2025-26 season, and he has not signed anywhere yet. Until a team gives him a role, he produces nothing for your roster. Cut, unless you have an open bench spot and can wait for him to sign somewhere.
